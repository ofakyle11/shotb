Post, VFX, Music & Release

@@CH20@@

Every chapter until now has been about making the picture. This one is about finishing it and getting it seen. Eight pages carry a production from the first assembly to the recoupment statement. Not one of them finishes a film. Each keeps the record that the people who do — the facility, the society, the programmer, the accountant — will ask you for.

On this chapter's accuracy. It was written by reading the eight modules' source, not by operating them. Cinamate is still being built, and where a page and this chapter disagree, the page is right and the chapter is the fault. This is a survey: what each module is for and where its edges are, not eight full tours.

The post schedule, and what actually happened

post/lib-post.js ships a fourteen-milestone template from assembly to delivery, each with a default working-day duration and a list of parents; one with several parents starts after the latest of them, so qc waits on the grade, the mix and the VFX finals. Dates are business days and nothing reads the clock. The calendar solves backward, landing delivery on the date you name, or forward from an assembly start; the two agree exactly. On the shipped template the critical path is 51 working days, through sound edit and mix rather than conform — which scripts/test_post.mjs pins.

Plan and truth are held apart, and that is the module's point. schedule() is a pure function of the template and the anchor; what happened lives in SB_PostActuals_v1, and overlay() lays one over the other to produce slip in signed business days, a forecast, and whether the critical path has moved now that observed durations replace estimates. One rule governs the actuals: a milestone marked done with no actual end date is not done. Readiness follows it — five deliverables (ProRes master, 5.1 printmaster, M&E, QC report, DCP) turn from planned to ready only on a real end date, because reporting an estimate as ready is how a distributor is promised a date nobody verified. Awarding a vendor bid in section 3 posts it to the Money Room as an open PO on account 15000, once.

Destructive, no confirmation. ↺ Clear actuals replaces the actuals store with a blank one: every recorded status and actual date is gone and every deliverable returns to planned. ↺ Template days drops your duration overrides. Neither asks first.

VFX: the bid, the award, and the PO number

vfx/lib-vfx.js scans the screenplay against fifteen cue patterns and proposes shots with a complexity tier attached. Shots are numbered VFX-010, VFX-020, ten past the highest on the board, and run briefed → bid → awarded → plates → temp → final → approved. Planning bands are per shot and labelled planning estimate wherever they appear: simple $500–1,500, medium $1,500–6,000, complex $6,000–20,000, hero $20,000–80,000. The plate checklist grows with the tier, from lens data and reference stills up to witness cam and a set survey.

The join. Awarding a shot writes a PO to the Money Room on account 15200 — the account the estimator budgets VFX to, so the budget line and the spend meet — and stores the PO number back on the shot as committedPo. That number is the only key matching the shot to its invoice. Entering the invoiced amount settles the PO and marks it paid; only then is an observation recorded, because an open PO is a commitment and not a cost. Two settled finals in a tier turn the correction on, and the band is multiplied by a weighted ratio of final to raw bid, clamped so no single overage can more than double a future band. Below that threshold the band is exactly as shipped and the page says how many shots it stands on, rather than a confident 1.00× over nothing.

"PO committed · unjoinable"

Boards written before that change stored true: the money was committed and the number thrown away. migratePoRefs runs on every render and tries to recover it, matching a non-void PO on 15200 whose description begins with the shot's code, never claiming a number twice. What it cannot match is counted as legacyPo and tagged PO committed · unjoinable. When you see it:

  • Read it as bookkeeping, not an error. The commitment is real and the bid still counts as awarded. What is lost is the chance to learn what that shot cost, so its tier's band stands on less evidence than your work.
  • Reconcile by hand. Compare the board's awarded total against the VFX POs on 15200. A tag with a matching PO under a different description is untidy; a tag with no PO at all is a commitment that never reached the ledger.
  • Do not delete the shot to clear the tag, and do not re-award it. The board is the only record tying that money to that shot, and a second award writes a second PO for the same work.
  • The flag heals itself. Should a matching PO appear on 15200 later, the next render claims it and the join is live again. Shots awarded by the current build are unaffected.

Music: the cue sheet is a deliverable, not a summary

music/lib-music.js scans for music moments — sings, karaoke, a band playing, a jukebox, a radio, humming, a quoted title — and proposes cues with a suggested use. Estimates run per side, sync and master both required except for library tracks and their one all-in fee: library $50–500, indie $500–5,000, known artist $5,000–25,000, famous $25,000–250,000, all-media. Featured use prices the top half of a tier's range, background the bottom half, titles at 1.5× the midpoint; festival-only scope is roughly 15% of the all-media figure, with a standing note to negotiate the step-up before you need it. Every figure is labelled a planning estimate by the code producing it. A cue set to licensed with an actual quote commits that amount to the Money Room on account 15000, once.

The module exists because of what it replaced. The office register wrote a nine-column sheet with one composer and one publisher per cue, no shares, no ISWC and no ISRC — and no performing-rights society will process that. A society pays writers and publishers by name, in stated percentages, routed through their affiliations. Those rows are not discarded: ↧ Import the office cue register reads SB_CueSheet_v1 and turns each into a writer and a publisher with an unstated share, which the pre-submission check then asks you to fill in.

What cueSheetIssues flags, and why each is a rejection

  • No timing — no TC in/out and no duration. A cue with no duration is not payable; the duration is what the royalty is calculated against.
  • No writers. A PRO pays writers, not a "composer" text field. There is nobody to pay.
  • No publishers. Enter Unpublished where the writer self-publishes; the publisher's half is addressed either way.
  • Shares not totalling 100% on either side, reported with the actual total. A society cannot divide a payment among percentages that do not close, and an unstated share is the commonest reason a writer is never paid for a cue that aired.
  • A writer row with no name, and any writer or named publisher with no PRO affiliation — without the society the royalty has nowhere to route.
  • No ISWC for the composition, and no ISRC where the cue names an artist. Blank is accepted but slows matching; the publisher and the label hold them.
  • No title, or a cue still called Untitled cue.
  • A cue that is not licensed. Only licensed cues belong on a delivered sheet. The check is waivable for a working draft; delivery is not.

Replaced cues are excluded from sheet and issue list alike. The preview prints NOT READY TO SUBMIT above the itemised list rather than looking finished, and the CSV writes one line per writer and publisher share, which is how a society ingests it. A cell opening with =, +, - or @ is prefixed with an apostrophe, so a cue title is never executed as a spreadsheet formula.

One gap, stated plainly. The rights gate in distribution/lib-dist.js honours a noMusic flag on SB_Music_v1 — a picture declaring it carries no third-party music. Nothing in music/index.html writes that flag, so it could not be established from source how an operator sets it.

Festivals: a premiere you can only spend once

festivals/lib-fest.js carries fourteen curated majors across four tiers, each with an approximate season window, a premiere note and a fee hint, under a banner saying windows and fees drift every year and must be verified before planning. No festival URL is hard-coded; the directory offers a search link instead. Strategy turns on one question — where has the film premiered? Unpremiered keeps every tier open, with the standing warning not to burn the world premiere on a minor festival while an A-list answer is pending. A spent US premiere drops the A-list; a spent world premiere pivots toward markets and buyers.

Deadlines in the tracker are the ones you entered and verified yourself. Fees are stored as the text you typed, verbatim, and read on the way out: "$1,200", "2 500" and "CAD 95" all parse, while "tbd" or "65–110" is reported as unknown by name rather than silently counted as zero.

Distribution: the schedule, the windows, and the door

distribution/lib-dist.js holds a 23-item delivery schedule in five groups — Picture, Audio, Accessibility, Marketing, Legal & docs — and five buyer presets deciding which items are required: festival premiere, theatrical, streamer (everything), aggregator/TVOD, broadcast. The windows planner records territory, channel, term and licensee, and flags an exclusivity clash only where all three collide at once — USA and domestic being one place, Nordics expanding to its members. A grant that does not state both ends of its term is reported as undated, naming the missing edge, rather than guessed at.

Screeners go out through a gate. rightsGate reads the cue list from SB_Music_v1 and the findings from SB_ClearScan_v1 and answers clear, caution, unknown or blocked. An unlicensed cue blocks; a festival-only licence blocks an all-media send and cautions a festival one; an open high-risk finding blocks. A store nobody has opened is unknown, not clear — absence of evidence is not clearance, and a missing gate is treated as an unchecked one rather than an open door. Sending anyway is possible and is recorded: the override reason must be at least three characters and contain a letter or digit, so a signature made of whitespace is refused, and the reason, the level and the blockers are written onto the row beside the recipient and the cut they received.

Screening: notes against a named cut

The Screening Room hosts no video. Every viewer opens the same exported cut file on their own machine and only the notes travel. A note is a timecode, text capped at 500 characters, an author and an open/done status, belonging to a session that records which cut it was made against — so notes from a v3 review are never read against v5.

Overwrites without asking. → Send open notes to Editor writes the open notes into SB_Cut_v1 as project.markers, replacing whatever markers were there.

Investors: the waterfall, run exactly

investors/lib-invest.js runs the standard indie recoupment order and reconciles to the cent: sales fee (default 15%) and off-the-top expenses first, then debt principal plus simple interest, then gap, then equity principal plus premium (default 20%), then remaining profit split 50/50 between producer and investor pool. That pool is shared by explicit backend percentages where any are set, otherwise pro-rata by amount, the rounding residue landing on the largest share. Shortfalls inside a tier pay pro-rata by what each is owed. The quarterly letter pulls the budget total and the Money Room's estimated final cost live from this browser, and where there is no data says so instead of inventing a figure. Every output repeats the caution: this is a model, not an audited accounting.

The incentive decoder

The Tax Credit Ledger reads the Money Room, separates open POs (committed) from invoiced, paid and petty cash (actual), and tags each row qualified or not. Explicit tags always win; untagged rows fall to a labelled heuristic guessing travel, lodging, insurance, bond, legal, financing and out-of-jurisdiction spend non-qualifying, everything else qualifying. The estimated credit is qualified spend times the midpoint of the jurisdiction's rate band, shown beside the Advisor's whole-budget model — budget × qualPct × midpoint — and the delta between them. That qualPct haircut belongs to the model and is applied exactly once, never to the ledger, which has real rows to look at. Below a program's minimum spend the estimate is zero; above its budget cap the page flags it.

An estimate from a table, and nothing more. taxcredit/lib-taxcred.js carries 21 jurisdiction entries mirrored verbatim from the Advisor's curated table of published program terms, 2025-26 vintage: a label, an effective rate band, the fraction of a typical budget assumed to qualify, and sometimes a minimum spend or budget cap. Georgia reads there as a 20–30% band on 75% of a typical budget with a $500,000 floor; British Columbia as a flat 36% labour-only credit on 45%. Those are the figures the table held on the day it was written, and programs change rates, caps and definitions every legislative session. This page is not an application, not a filing and not advice: it produces a number to plan against and a checklist that defers, in its own words, to your accountant. Heritage Gold is the kit's accent for a tax-credit win, and the win is worth marking — but what is marked is an estimate.

What the platform deliberately does not do

This is the last chapter, so it is the place to be plain about the edges.

  • It does not master. No DCP writer, no IMF packager, no conform, no printmaster, no QC engine. DCP mastering is a milestone on a calendar and a line on a checklist — records of work a facility does.
  • It does not file anything. The tax-credit checklist does not register a production or submit a claim. The cue sheet is a CSV you deliver; nothing is transmitted to a society. Submissions are logged here and entered on the festival's own platform.
  • It does not bank. No money moves. A PO is a row in SB_Money_v1, a waterfall run is arithmetic, and an investor statement is a document you send.
  • It does not give legal advice. The rights gate reports what your own records say and refuses a send on that basis. It does not clear a right, read a contract, or know what your agreements permit.
  • It does not make deals. No marketplace, no buyer introductions, no sales agency. The windows planner records terms somebody else negotiated and says when two of them collide.

The hand-offs follow. A post facility and a sound house issue the masters. A society processes the cue sheet, and a music supervisor or clearance lawyer secures the licences behind it. A film accountant files the incentive claim from the qualified-spend ledger. A sales agent takes the film to buyers, and an entertainment lawyer papers what they agree. Your collection-account manager, not this page, tells your investors what they have actually been paid.

What Cinamate does is hold the record in one shape, in one place, so that when each of those professionals asks what you have, the answer is a document rather than a search of your inbox. That is the whole of its ambition here, and it is enough. Begin your next production.
