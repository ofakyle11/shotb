Safety, Clearance & Deal Memos

@@CH14@@

Three modules that exist to keep a production legal and unhurt. They share one habit: read the screenplay you already have, name the thing that could go wrong, and put a person's title against it. None of them decides anything. Each produces paper a professional — a coordinator, a lawyer, an underwriter — is meant to read next.

On the accuracy of this chapter. Every behaviour described here was established by reading the files this page names, not by operating the software. The platform is under active development; where this chapter and the running code disagree, the code is what runs.
None of this is legal advice, and the platform says so itself. production/lib-prod.js states in its own header that its rates and lists "follow widely published conventions and are estimates, not legal or accounting advice". The three modules in this chapter say the same thing in their own words: the safety page tells you to confirm real rates with the police service and calls its animal figures planning estimates; contracts/lib-deal.js says counsel reviews the memo before signature. A clearance report is a prompt for counsel, not a substitute for one, and an E&O readiness banner is a checklist the production filled in about itself — not a policy, and not an underwriter's opinion. If you are about to shoot a stunt, licence a song, or ask someone to sign something, these pages are where that conversation begins, not where it ends.

Where hazards come from

Safety & Risk is safety/index.html over safety/lib-safety.js (CSafety), stored in SB_Safety_v1. Assess the script reads the working screenplay from SB_Timeline_v1.scriptText — under about forty characters it declines and says so — and splits it through the one scene model, js/lib-scenes.js. Safety scans real scenes only: a title page or a FADE IN: preamble is not a scene and is not assessed.

Each scene's slugline and body are then tested against a fixed table of eleven hazards. The table is closed: a danger it does not name is not found, and nothing on this page adds one.

The source describes the three tiers as 3 stop-and-plan, 2 supervised, 1 note-and-brief; they print as HIGH, MED and LOW. A scene's score is the sum of its hazard severities, the production's risk score the sum of the scene scores — a way of sorting the schedule, not a measurement of anything.

Matching is by keyword over the scene text, which cuts both ways. A weapon somebody only mentions in dialogue is still flagged — the safe direction — but the night-exterior test wants an EXT slugline carrying NIGHT, DUSK or DAWN, and misses one written any other way. An empty hazard list means the words were not there, not that the day is safe.

The risk assessment document

Section 2 renders the printable assessment: production, preparer, date, then one block per flagged scene — every hazard at its tier, the responsible person named against it, and the standard control measures beneath, down to the safety boat and throw lines in position before rehearsal for water work. It closes with the deduplicated list of specialist personnel the schedule needs, and the line that all hazards are to be re-assessed on the day by the 1st AD at the safety meeting, then downloads as risk-assessment.txt for the call-sheet pack.

The document regenerates over your edits. The textarea is writable, but re-running Assess the script replaces its contents — and so does typing in Prepared by, which re-runs the assessment on every keystroke. Set the name first, then edit, then copy out.

Officers, animals and the day

Section 3 names which scenes need police — an exterior driving scene, weapons visible in a public exterior, any crowd scene, street stunts — and prices the booking at officers × hours × rate × days plus admin, defaulting to a four-hour minimum call, $90 an hour and 10% admin over a caption telling you to confirm real rates with the service. Its directory of twelve production hubs refuses to invent contact details: only Toronto carries a request page, the rest name the service and its program with no URL at all, and an unknown city returns nothing rather than a guess.

Section 3b covers animals, costing seven species bands at the midpoint of a day-rate range plus a wrangler across shoot and prep days. Exotic and big animals carry the note that they are heavily restricted or banned in many jurisdictions and need legal review before scheduling. The nine-item compliance checklist, following the certified animal-safety monitoring standard, lets only the coordinator handle the animals, bans sedation and tripping devices, requires a current veterinary exam, and puts the animal action on the call sheet.

Both commit buttons post every time you press them. → Commit to Money Room writes a fresh open purchase order into SB_Money_v1 on each click, with no guard against duplicates — paid duty to account 3000, animals to 9000. Two clicks are two commitments; correct a mistake in the Money Room, not here.

Section 4 builds the morning safety meeting: exits, muster point and medic location first, then one line per hazard naming who confirms the controls are in place — scoped to today's scene numbers, or blank for the whole schedule. Section 5 is the incident log, with an injury flag so a near-miss records as a near-miss and a reminder that injuries need the insurer notified. scripts/test_safety.mjs covers all of this and names one open gap rather than hiding it: hazards are reported by numeric scene, so a meeting scoped to scene 1 also returns scene 1A's hazards. Over-inclusive — the safe direction for a safety document, but wrong.

Clearance: the pre-E&O script read

Clearance is clearance/index.html over clearance/lib-clear.js (CClear), stored in SB_ClearScan_v1. It scans every scene for eight categories of finding, each with a risk tier and the standard action:

CategoryRiskStandard action
Music referenceshighSync + master licence before the cut locks, or original score
Phone numbershighReplace with 555-01XX or a number the production controls
Broadcast / archival footagehighLicence the material or shoot an original insert
Real peoplehighLegal review — consent, or a clear public-figure basis
Brands and marksmediumPlacement agreement, greek the mark, or swap for a cleared fiction
On-screen artworkmediumClear the underlying work or dress with production-owned art
Currency reproductionmediumProp money compliant with reproduction rules
SignagelowVerify fictional or greeked; clear any real storefront mark

Phone numbers are the one detector that filters itself: any number containing 555 is passed over, everything else is reported. Each finding carries its scene, an excerpt, and a status you set — pending, cleared, rewritten or accepted risk. Re-scanning after a rewrite preserves statuses already set against a matching category, scene and term.

Read the findings knowing how they are made. The brand list is one long string split on spaces, so multi-word marks became separate single-word tokens: King, Louis, Red, Target, Apple, Camel and Express all sit in the list alone, and any capitalised occurrence is flagged. Matching is case-sensitive, which suits a screenplay that writes marks in caps but also catches an ordinary word opening a sentence. The other detectors capture to the end of the line, so a term often trails into the next sentence. And unlike safety, clearance scans the preamble, reporting anything before the first slugline as scene 0. The module over-reports rather than misses: expect false positives and clear them deliberately.

E&O readiness, and what it is not

The banner above the findings answers one question: is this file fit to go to an underwriter? The source is blunt about why its rule is strict — "E&O-ready is a representation made to an insurer" — and about having twice shipped green when it should not have. As now implemented, ready means every script finding is cleared or rewritten, and each of four files has been read and says yes: the clearance scan itself, the chain of title in SB_Rights_v1, the cue sheet in SB_Music_v1, and the E&O certificate in SB_Insurance_v1. Anything else is a blocker with a name.

Three things to know before you argue with that banner. Accepted risk is a decision to carry a finding, not to clear it: it blocks readiness and is listed separately under "disclose on the application", because the underwriter decides, not the production. An unknown is a blocker in its own right — a store nobody opened, a policy row missing carrier, number or expiry, a scan that never ran — on the stated principle that absence of evidence is not clearance, and that a blank expiry is not a policy that never expires. And zero findings is a clean read only if a scan is on record. Beyond the findings, the chain of title needs one executed underlying-rights agreement — an option, a purchase, life rights or a writer agreement — while cues licensed for festivals only pass as a disclosure with a reminder to exercise the step-up before delivery, and a picture with no third-party music can say so on the record in Music. scripts/test_ops_rights.mjs pins the rule against the exact states which once reported ready.

A gap between the page and the engine. The engine deliberately distinguishes "read and empty" from "never read". The page, in its eoContext, substitutes an empty list for a missing rights or insurance store. It still blocks — an empty register produces "no executed underlying-rights agreement" and "no E&O policy on file" — so the banner does not go green falsely. But those blockers are not counted as unknowns, and the summary's "everything was actually read" flag can read true when nothing was. Judge readiness from the named blockers, not that flag.

The lower half of the page drafts paper: a materials permission request, a sync licence request, a location release and an appearance release, each filled with the active production name. All four are short-form plain-language drafts. Send them to counsel before you send them to a rights holder.

Deal memos

Deal Memos is contracts/index.html over contracts/lib-deal.js (CDeal), stored in SB_Deals_v1. Draft from Crew register reads SB_Crew_v1 and creates a memo for every crew row that lacks one, at a day rate with five guaranteed days and 1.5× after twelve hours on set. Cast agreements start at the SAG-AFTRA theatrical basic scale the module holds in one place — $1,204 a day, $4,181 a week — with three guaranteed days and $60 per diem. An NDA template sits alongside.

The memo states production, name and role, union or guild, rate and basis, guarantee, start date, overtime terms, kit rental, per diem, and for cast the credit position, closing on standard terms: safety compliance, confidentiality, and work-made-for-hire assignment of results and proceeds. A SAG-AFTRA rate is checked against scale in the document itself — the old test passed anything at or below scale, so a $600 day rate, half the minimum, printed "(scale)". A rate under the minimum now prints in full that a signatory production may not pay it, and to confirm the applicable agreement.

The value against each row is not the fee. It is what a signed deal obligates: fee, an overtime allowance of 12% unless the terms read flat or all-in, payroll fringes on both, plus kit and per diem, which are not fringed. Fringes follow the union — 40% for IATSE and Teamsters, 36% for DGA and WGA, 28% for SAG-AFTRA and as the non-union default — and per diem is paid per working day, so a weekly guarantee pays five. A cast agreement at scale for three days comes to $5,358.16: $3,612 fee, $433.44 overtime, $1,132.72 fringes, $180 per diem.

Mark a deal signed and that full value posts to the Money Room as an open purchase order, on the account the platform's one chart gives the role — a gaffer to 8000, a performer to 4000 — with the breakdown in the PO notes. That is the point: the cost report sees payroll the moment ink lands, at loaded cost rather than the headline rate.

What the memo is, and is not. It is a short form, and it says of itself that it binds both parties on signature and is superseded by a long-form agreement if one is issued. Read that plainly: this template does not describe itself as non-binding, which is exactly why both the module and the page say counsel reviews it before signature. It is not a guild agreement and does not incorporate one, and the scale figures in it are one stored pair of numbers, not a live schedule.
Deleting a deal does not undo its commitment. Removing a row asks for confirmation, then drops it from SB_Deals_v1 — but the purchase order it raised stays open in SB_Money_v1. Reverting a signed deal to draft leaves it standing too, and editing the rate afterwards does not revise it. Void or correct the PO in the Money Room yourself.
