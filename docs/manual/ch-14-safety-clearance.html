Safety, Clearance & Deal Memos

@@CH14@@

Three modules that exist to keep a production legal and unhurt. They share one habit: read the screenplay you already have, name the thing that could go wrong, and put a person's title against it. None of them makes a decision for you. Each one produces paper that a professional — a coordinator, a lawyer, an underwriter — is meant to read next.

On the accuracy of this chapter. Every behaviour described here was established by reading the files named on this page, not by operating the software. The platform is under active development; where this chapter and the running code disagree, the code is what runs.
None of this is legal advice, and the platform says so itself. production/lib-prod.js states in its own header that its rates and lists "follow widely published conventions and are estimates, not legal or accounting advice", and the same standard governs this whole slice. A clearance report is a prompt for counsel, not a substitute for one. An E&O readiness banner is a checklist the production filled in about itself, not a policy and not an underwriter's opinion. A deal memo is a plain-language template — contracts/lib-deal.js says "counsel reviews before signature", and it means before, not after. Every rate, fee and percentage in these three modules is an editable planning figure. If you are about to shoot a stunt, licence a song, or ask someone to sign something, the output of these pages is the beginning of that conversation.

Where hazards come from

Safety & Risk is safety/index.html over safety/lib-safety.js (CSafety), with its own store in SB_Safety_v1. Assess the script reads the working screenplay from SB_Timeline_v1.scriptText — under about forty characters it declines and says so — and splits it through the one scene model, js/lib-scenes.js. Safety scans real scenes only: a title page or a FADE IN: preamble is not a scene and is not assessed.

Each scene's slugline and body are then tested against a fixed table of eleven hazards. The table is closed. A danger it does not name is not found, and nothing in this page lets you add one.

The three tiers are described in the source as 3 stop-and-plan, 2 supervised, 1 note-and-brief, and they print on the document as HIGH, MED and LOW. A scene's score is the sum of its hazard severities; the production's risk score is the sum of the scene scores. Treat both as a way of sorting the schedule, not as a measurement of anything.

Matching is by keyword over the scene text, which cuts both ways. A weapon somebody only talks about in dialogue is still flagged — the safe direction. But the night-exterior test looks for an EXT slugline carrying NIGHT, DUSK or DAWN, so a night exterior written any other way is missed. An empty hazard list means the words were not there, not that the day is safe.

The risk assessment document

Section 2 renders the printable assessment: production name, preparer, date, and one block per flagged scene — every hazard at its tier, the responsible person named against it, and the standard control measures beneath. Water work, for instance, carries the safety boat and throw lines in position before rehearsal, cast swim ability confirmed in writing, water temperature and current assessed daily, and a rewarming plan. The document closes with the deduplicated list of specialist personnel the whole schedule needs, and the line that all hazards are to be re-assessed on the day by the 1st AD at the safety meeting. Copy puts it on the clipboard; the download button writes risk-assessment.txt for the call-sheet pack.

The document regenerates over your edits. The textarea is writable, but re-running Assess the script replaces its whole contents — and so does typing in the Prepared by field, which re-runs the assessment on every keystroke. Set the name first, then edit, then copy out. Anything typed into the box is lost on the next scan.

Paid duty police and the animal department

Section 3 answers which scenes need officers and roughly what that costs. Four conditions trigger a requirement: an exterior driving scene (traffic control), weapons visible in a public exterior (police notification plus an on-set officer), any crowd scene, and street stunts (road closure supervision). The directory holds twelve production hubs, and it refuses to invent contact details — Toronto carries the Toronto Police Service paid-duty request page; every other entry names the service and its program with no URL at all, and the page offers a search instead. An unknown city returns nothing rather than a guess. Incentive jurisdictions map to their hub, so the Use Advisor location button reads SB_Budget_v1.incentive and resolves, for example, Ontario to Toronto.

The estimate is officers × hours × rate × days, plus an admin percentage — defaults are one officer, a four-hour minimum call, $90 an hour and 10% admin, and the page's own caption says typical terms carry a three to four hour minimum and that you must confirm exact rates with the service. A short day is billed at the minimum call.

Section 3b covers animals. Seven species bands carry day-rate ranges; the estimate takes midpoints for the animal across shoot days, a wrangler across shoot and prep days, and a vet across shoot days only. Exotic and big animals carry the note that they are heavily restricted or banned in many jurisdictions and need legal review before scheduling; venomous reptiles carry their own. The compliance checklist — nine items, following the certified animal-safety monitoring standard — requires that only the coordinator handles the animals, bans sedation and tripping devices, requires a current veterinary exam, agrees take limits before rolling, and puts the animal action on the call sheet. The coordinator directory names exactly one house it can stand behind and sends you to a search for the rest.

Both commit buttons post every time you press them. → Commit to Money Room writes a fresh open purchase order into SB_Money_v1 on each click, with no guard against duplicates — paid duty lands on account 3000, animals on 9000. Two clicks are two commitments. Correct a mistake in the Money Room, not here.

The meeting and the incident log

Section 4 builds the morning safety meeting from the same analysis. It always opens with exits, muster point and medic location, then adds one line per hazard naming who confirms the controls are in place. Give it today's scene numbers to scope it to the day; leave it blank for the whole schedule. Section 5 is the incident log, held in SB_Safety_v1: date, person, what happened, action taken and an injury flag, so a near-miss records as a near-miss. On logging, the page reminds you that injuries also need the insurer notified.

Clearance: the pre-E&O script read

Clearance is clearance/index.html over clearance/lib-clear.js (CClear), stored in SB_ClearScan_v1. It scans every scene for eight categories of finding, each with a risk tier and the standard action:

CategoryRiskStandard action
Music referenceshighSync + master licence before the cut locks, or original score
Phone numbershighReplace with 555-01XX or a number the production controls
Broadcast / archival footagehighLicence the material or shoot an original insert
Real peoplehighLegal review — consent, or a clear public-figure basis
Brands and marksmediumPlacement agreement, greek the mark, or swap for a cleared fiction
On-screen artworkmediumClear the underlying work or dress with production-owned art
Currency reproductionmediumProp money compliant with reproduction rules
SignagelowVerify fictional or greeked; clear any real storefront mark

Phone numbers are the one detector that filters itself: a 555 number is not reported, everything else is. Each finding carries its scene, an excerpt and a status you set — pending, cleared, rewritten or accepted risk. Re-scanning after a rewrite preserves statuses already set on a matching category, scene and term.

Read the findings knowing how they are made. The brand list is a single string split on spaces, so multi-word marks became separate one-word tokens: King, Louis, Red, Target, Apple, Camel and Express are all in the list on their own, and any capitalised occurrence of them is flagged as a brand. Matching is case-sensitive, which suits a screenplay that writes marks in caps but also catches an ordinary word at the head of a sentence. The other detectors capture to the end of the line, so a finding's term often trails into the next sentence. And unlike safety, clearance scans the preamble too, reporting anything before the first slugline as scene 0. Expect false positives and clear them deliberately; the module is built to over-report rather than miss.

E&O readiness, and what it is not

The banner above the findings answers one question: is this production ready to make representations to an insurer? The source is blunt about why the rule is strict — "E&O-ready is a representation made to an insurer", and it has twice shipped green when it should not have. The rule now stated in the code, and implemented exactly: ready means every script finding is cleared or rewritten, and each of four files has been read and says yes. Those four are the clearance scan itself, the chain of title in SB_Rights_v1, the cue sheet in SB_Music_v1, and the E&O certificate in SB_Insurance_v1. Anything else is a named blocker.

Three consequences worth knowing before you argue with the banner. Accepted risk is a decision to carry a finding, not to clear it: it blocks readiness and it is separately listed under "disclose on the application", because the underwriter decides, not the production. An unknown is a blocker in its own right — a store nobody opened, a policy row with no carrier, number or expiry, a scan that never ran — on the stated principle that absence of evidence is not clearance; a blank expiry is not a policy that never expires. And zero findings only means a clean read if a scan is on record. A chain of title needs an executed underlying-rights agreement: an option, a purchase, life rights or a writer agreement. Cues licensed for festivals only are allowed through as a disclosure with a reminder to exercise the step-up before delivery, and a picture with no third-party music can say so on the record in the Music module.

A gap between the page and the engine. The engine deliberately distinguishes "read and empty" from "never read". The page, in its eoContext, substitutes an empty list for a missing rights or insurance store before handing it over. The result still blocks — an empty register produces "no executed underlying-rights agreement" and "no E&O policy on file" — so the banner does not go green falsely. But those blockers are not counted as unknowns, and the summary's "everything was actually read" flag can read true when nothing was. Judge readiness from the named blockers, not from that flag.

The second half of the page drafts the paper: a materials permission request, a synchronization licence request, a location release and an appearance release, each filled with the active production name. They are short-form plain-language drafts. Send them to counsel before you send them to a rights holder.

Deal memos

Deal Memos is contracts/index.html over contracts/lib-deal.js (CDeal), stored in SB_Deals_v1. Draft from Crew register reads SB_Crew_v1 and creates a memo for every crew row that does not have one, defaulting to a day rate, five guaranteed days and 1.5× after twelve hours on set. Cast agreements start at the SAG-AFTRA theatrical basic scale the module holds in one place — $1,204 a day, $4,181 a week — with three guaranteed days and $60 per diem. There is also an NDA template.

The memo states the production, name and role, union or guild, rate and basis, guarantee, start date, overtime terms, kit or box rental, per diem, and for cast the credit position. It closes on standard terms: safety compliance, confidentiality, and work-made-for-hire assignment of results and proceeds.

A rate on a SAG-AFTRA deal is checked against scale and annotated in the document itself. The source explains why the check is strict: the old test passed anything at or below scale, so a $600 day rate — half the minimum — printed "(scale)". A rate below the minimum now prints in full that a signatory production may not pay it and to confirm the applicable agreement.

The value shown against each row is not the fee. It is what a signed deal obligates: fee, an overtime allowance of 12% unless the terms read flat or all-in, payroll fringes on both, plus kit and per diem, which are not fringed. Fringes follow the union — 40% for IATSE and Teamsters, 36% for DGA, WGA, 28% for SAG-AFTRA and as the non-union default — and per diem is paid per working day, so a weekly guarantee pays five. A cast agreement at scale for three days comes to $5,358.16: $3,612 fee, $433.44 overtime allowance, $1,132.72 fringes and $180 per diem.

Mark a deal signed and that full value posts to the Money Room as an open purchase order, on the account the platform's one chart gives the role — a gaffer to 8000, a lead performer to 4000 — with the breakdown written into the PO notes. The point of the exercise is that the cost report sees payroll the moment ink lands, at its true loaded cost rather than the headline rate.

What the memo is, and is not. It is a short form, not a long form, and it says of itself that it binds both parties on signature and is superseded by a long-form agreement if one is issued. Read that plainly: this template does not describe itself as non-binding, which is precisely why the module and the page both say counsel reviews before signature. It is not a guild agreement, it does not incorporate one, and the scale figures in it are a single stored pair of numbers, not a live schedule. Confirm the applicable agreement before anyone signs.
Deleting a deal does not undo its commitment. Removing a row asks for confirmation and then drops it from SB_Deals_v1 — but the purchase order it raised stays open in SB_Money_v1. Reverting a signed deal to draft leaves it standing too, and editing the rate afterwards does not revise it. Void or correct the PO in the Money Room yourself.

Testing

Two node suites cover this ground and are worth knowing about, because they document the failures the modules are shaped around. scripts/test_safety.mjs exercises the hazard table, the assessment, the meeting checklist, the police directory — including that an unknown city returns null rather than a guess and that no entry invents a phone number — the animal department and the incident log. Its fixture deliberately carries a preamble and an A-scene, and it names one open gap rather than hiding it: hazards are reported by numeric scene, so a meeting scoped to scene 1 also returns scene 1A's hazards. Over-inclusive, which is the safe direction for a safety document, but wrong. scripts/test_ops_rights.mjs pins the readiness rule against the exact states that once reported ready: every finding flipped to accepted risk, an E&O row with no carrier and no expiry, a music store nobody ever opened.
